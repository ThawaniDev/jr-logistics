**The most important sentence:** with payment on the 5th of the next month, **your 50,000 SAR is not enough.** Every month's second driver payout (on the last day) leaves **5 days before** that month's warehouse money arrives (on the 5th), so the business has to pre-fund one month of driver pay, and that month grows 30% every month. The 50,000 is used up on 31 January and the shortfall reaches **481,752 SAR on 31 December**. Each shortfall lasts only **5 days** and is closed on the 5th by the warehouse payment; the cheapest fix is to pay the second half of the drivers on the 6th instead of the last day (section 7): then 50,000 SAR is enough for the whole year.

Read it like this: on the 5th the previous month's billing arrives, on the 15th the first half is paid, on the last day the second half is paid. A negative amount in "Cash on the last day" is money you must have available that day (a bank line, a partner loan or extra capital) until the warehouses pay on the 5th. The "Cash on the 1st" of a month is the "Cash on the last day" of the month before.

### How the capital is used

* **15 Jan 2027:** the first payout, 31,200, is paid from the capital: 18,800 is left (62% used).
* **31 Jan 2027:** the second payout, 31,200, is bigger than what is left, so you are **12,400 short** until the warehouses pay on 5 Feb.
* After that the capital is never back at 50,000 as a buffer: each month the money that arrives on the 5th (last month's billing) pays the 15th, and the second payout is again paid before the next money arrives.

* The shortfall happens only **between the last day of the month and the 5th (5 days)**. On every 5th the warehouse money brings the cash back above zero, and the payout on the 15th is covered.

* In total you are below zero for **60 days** in the year (12 windows of 5 days).

## 8. Not in these numbers (they reduce the margin)

* **VAT 15%.** If 7 SAR is the price before VAT, the platform collects VAT with the invoice and pays it to ZATCA: cash-neutral, but it must be kept apart from the capital (and the warehouses' VAT arrives on the 5th too). If 7 SAR already includes VAT, the margin is 1 SAR minus the VAT on the whole 7 SAR.
* **Running costs:** team, office, software and hosting, SMS (Msegat), card fees (PayTabs), insurance, marketing, driver recruitment and equipment. They have to be paid from the same cash, and they widen the shortfalls above.
* **Zakat / income tax**, bad debts, disputed or failed deliveries, and shipments the driver delivers but the warehouse disputes (a warehouse that pays late moves its money past the 5th: see the table in section 7, D).
* **Margin is small in the first months** (10,400 SAR in January, 13,520 in February): every 10,000 SAR of monthly fixed cost needs 10,000 shipments a month just to break even at 1 SAR each.

## 9. How this maps to the JR platform

* The platform **today collects through a prepaid wallet** (the warehouse's balance is reserved when an order is published and charged on delivery). **Paying on the 5th of the next month is the credit model:** a company can be given a **credit limit** (its balance may go below zero up to that amount). Each warehouse's limit must cover **about one month plus 5 days of its billing** (the largest amount it owes just before the 5th).
* **Not built yet:** a monthly statement per warehouse, due dates (the 5th) and overdue handling. They are listed as gap L6 in `memory/audit/gaps.md`. This plan needs them.
* **Driver payouts every 15 days:** the platform credits a driver's wallet as soon as the job is completed, and the driver asks to withdraw (minimum 100 SAR by default). A 15-day settlement is therefore a **payout routine, not an automatic schedule**: on the payout days Finance exports the withdrawal requests (Finance › Withdrawals, bank file) and marks the batch paid. There is no scheduler that pays by itself. Moving the second payout to the 6th (option A) is only a change in that routine.
* **If warehouses prepaid** their wallet before the work (the current model), the warehouse money would already be in the bank when drivers are paid and the working capital needed would be close to zero.
* The 7 / 6 / 1 split is set in **Finance › Pricing**: customer price 7, provider cost 6 per shipment; the margin is the difference.
